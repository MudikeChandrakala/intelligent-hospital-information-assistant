Patients are encouraged to confirm the exact consultation timing for their chosen doctor or department at the time of booking, either through the reception desk, appointment helpline, or online scheduling system, since timings can vary across departments and may be revised periodically based on patient volume and staffing.

FAQ-018
Question:

Answer:
Yes, the hospital operates a licensed blood bank responsible for the collection, testing, storage, and safe distribution of blood and blood components such as packed red cells, platelets, plasma, and cryoprecipitate. All donated units undergo rigorous screening for infectious diseases and blood group compatibility before being released for transfusion. The blood bank works in coordination with treating physicians to ensure timely availability of compatible blood products for patients undergoing surgery, trauma care, or treatment for chronic blood disorders.

FAQ-019
Question:
How can I donate blood at the hospital?
Answer:
Voluntary blood donation can be carried out at the hospital's blood bank during its designated donation hours. Prospective donors are required to meet standard eligibility criteria relating to age, body weight, hemoglobin level, and overall health status, which are assessed through a brief medical screening prior to donation. Donors are also asked about recent travel history, medication use, and any existing medical conditions to ensure both donor safety and the suitability of the donated blood for transfusion.
--------------------------------------------------
FAQ-020
Question:
What is the process for arranging blood for a patient?
Answer:
When a patient requires a blood transfusion, the treating physician issues a requisition specifying the required blood group, component, and quantity needed. Family members or attendants can submit this requisition at the blood bank, where compatibility testing, known as cross-matching, is performed before the blood unit is released. In cases of shortage for a rare blood group, the blood bank may coordinate with voluntary donors, replacement donation drives, or other blood banks to arrange the required units promptly.
